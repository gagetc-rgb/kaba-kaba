Questions

What buyers ask first.

Can a foreigner hold this land?

Yes, as a leaseholder. The freehold title stays with the Indonesian owner, as the law requires, and you hold a notarial lease. You see the full lease terms before any deposit.

What happens in 2056?

The lease runs to 2056, and an extension of up to 30 years is guaranteed in it, at the market rate at the time. As is standard in Bali, permanent buildings stay with the land when the lease ends.

What if the owner dies or sells the land?

The lease continues. It binds the owner’s heirs, and a sale of the land does not end it.

Who handles the permits?

Your architect prepares the design; the landowner signs the application and gathers the approvals from the neighbours and the village. The costs are yours.

Is it safe by the river?

The three plots sit 7 to 21 m above the riverbed, and its cross-sections are in the document pack for your engineer. The ITR places the area in a flood-hazard category, so your building permit sets the exact setback from the water.

Can I rent it out?

Living here yourself and long-term letting are straightforward. Nightly letting depends on a business licence, not on zoning: the ITR already lists villa use as permissible.
